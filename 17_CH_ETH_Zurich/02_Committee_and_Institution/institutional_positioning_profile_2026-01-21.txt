1. **Official name of the unit**
   **Scientific Integrity Office (SIO)** (also referred to as **Integrity Office** on ETH’s “Scientific misconduct” page). 

2. **Institutional location**
   Assigned **to the Executive Board domain of Research** (“for the purpose of administration”). 
   (ETH’s Annual Report also describes the “Executive Board domain of Research” as the domain in which scientific integrity structures are situated.) ([ETH Zürich][1])

3. **Core responsibilities**

* **Handling academic misconduct cases — explicitly mentioned**

  * Receives and processes **reports of suspected scientific misconduct**. 
  * Provides **technical and administrative support** to the Integrity Commission during investigation procedures (e.g., minutes, filing, support for hearings, drafting resolutions/reports). 
  * ETH states that **proceedings/investigations are conducted by the Integrity Commission**, which is supported by the SIO. ([ETH Zürich][2])

* **Promoting academic integrity — explicitly mentioned (as advice); broader “culture” work is described elsewhere**

  * Provides **advice on issues of scientific integrity**. 
  * **Not specified in official sources** that the SIO itself runs training/promotion programmes; however ETH’s **Scientific Integrity and Research Ethics** department states that its **Scientific Integrity Unit** supports the **Commission for Good Scientific Practice** in “establishing a culture of scientific integrity and good scientific practice.” ([ETH Zürich][3])

* **Overseeing integrity regulations / ordinance — partially explicitly mentioned**

  * The SIO’s duties include **monitoring international developments** in scientific integrity and **organising procedures** in cases of suspected misconduct. 
  * ETH’s **Scientific Integrity and Research Ethics** department states that it **ensures the development of guidelines** and the establishment/further development of suitable structures and processes (this is stated at department level, not specifically as an SIO duty). ([ETH Zürich][3])

Here is the **reporting & authority structure** for ETH Zurich’s scientific misconduct (research integrity) procedure, based **only on official ETH/Swiss sources**.

---

## 5. Reporting & authority structure

### a. Reports to

* **Integrity Commission (Integritätskommission / Integrity Commission):**
  **Not specified in official sources** as a formal “reporting line,” but it is explicitly stated that the Commission **provides recommendations for measures to the ETH Executive Board** (after investigating suspicions of misconduct). ([ETH Zürich][1])
  The Ordinance also specifies that the Commission transmits its **investigation report** to the **Executive Board committee**. 

* **Scientific Integrity Office (SIO):**
  The SIO is explicitly **assigned to the Executive Board domain of Research**. 
  Additionally, the **Vice President for Research** can **delegate other tasks** in scientific integrity / good scientific practice to the SIO. 
  Any further “reports to” relationship is **not specified in official sources**.

* **Executive Board committee (Executive Board committee):**
  This is an Executive Board-level body (Rector + relevant Vice Presidents; President involved in specific cases) and is not described as “reporting to” another ETH internal body in the Ordinance. 

### b. Decision-making authority

* **Integrity Commission:**
  The Ordinance explicitly assigns the Commission authority to **issue procedural rulings** and manage core procedural outputs (e.g., investigation report publication decisions), and to **recommend measures**. 
  It also states the Commission (Chair) represents the Commission in **appeal proceedings against independently contestable interim rulings**. 

* **Executive Board committee:**
  The Executive Board committee explicitly **hears the accused** regarding recommended measures and **describes the measures it considers necessary**, asking responsible ETH offices to implement them. 

**Overall (explicitly described in the Ordinance):**
A **mixed structure**: the **Commission investigates + issues procedural rulings + recommends measures**, while the **Executive Board committee determines/sets the measures to be implemented**. 

### c. Appeal or second-opinion mechanisms

* **Appeals (formal):**
  The Ordinance contains a dedicated **“Rights of appeal”** provision stating that **independently contestable interim rulings by the Commission** and **rulings of other ETH bodies** are governed by the **rights-of-appeal rules under Art. 37(3) of the ETH Act**. 
  An official ETH Zurich legal guidance document (“Information on Legal Action”) states (under Art. 37(3) ETH Act) that **appeals against ETH Zurich decrees may be lodged with the ETH Appeals Commission**, and that **appeals against decisions of the ETH Appeals Commission can be lodged with the Federal Administrative Court**. ([ETH Zürich][2])

* **Recusal as an internal procedural safeguard (second-opinion-type mechanism, explicitly stated):**
  If the **entire Commission** is requested to be recused, the **Executive Board decides** and may appoint a replacement commission. 

No ETH-internal “ombudsperson appeal route” for misconduct decisions is explicitly described on the ETH scientific misconduct page or in the cited Ordinance sections; therefore: **Not specified in official sources.**
