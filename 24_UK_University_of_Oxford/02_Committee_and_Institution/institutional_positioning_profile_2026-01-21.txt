I’ll look up Oxford’s official pages (policies/regulations and any “research integrity” or “academic misconduct” offices), then extract only what’s explicitly stated about where the function sits and what it does, with precise source references.


1. **Official name of the unit**
   **Research Strategy and Policy Unit (Research Services)** 
   *(Related, for formal allegations of research misconduct: **the Registrar** is the “senior named officer” responsible for responding to allegations.)* 

2. **Institutional location**
   **Located within Research Services** (as the “Research Strategy and Policy Unit (Research Services)”). 
   **Formal allegations of misconduct in research** are made in writing to **the Registrar**. ([University of Oxford][1])

3. **Core responsibilities**

* **Handling academic misconduct cases (explicitly mentioned for research misconduct):**

  * The **Registrar** has “designated responsibility for responding to allegations of misconduct in research” and determines next steps (e.g., whether to proceed to investigation / set up a panel). 
  * The **Director of the Research Strategy and Policy Unit (Research Services)** is named as a confidential contact “before any formal allegation is made.” 

* **Promoting academic integrity (explicitly mentioned for research integrity):**

  * Research integrity training is stated as **compulsory** for University research staff and research students, with training described and signposted via University research-support pages. ([University of Oxford][1])
  * The **Research and Innovation Committee** has “central responsibility for advising on policies which promote research integrity.” 

* **Overseeing integrity regulations / ordinance (explicitly mentioned):**

  * The **Academic Integrity in Research: Code of Practice and Procedure** is identified as the University’s code setting expectations/standards and the **formal procedure** for suspected misconduct in research. 
  * The **Research and Innovation Committee** approves (on behalf of Council) the University’s annual statement of compliance with the UK Concordat to Support Research Integrity. 

*(Student academic misconduct note, explicitly distinguished in Oxford’s research-misconduct code: concerns about students’ examined work fall under the Proctors’ jurisdiction, not the research-misconduct procedure.)* ([hr.admin.ox.ac.uk][2])

5. **Reporting & authority structure**

a. **Reports to**

* **Research and Innovation Committee**: A committee **reporting directly to University Council (or one of its main committees)**, and it explicitly **advises Council** and can **approve on behalf of Council** (e.g., the annual Concordat statement). ([governance.admin.ox.ac.uk][1])
* **Registrar (misconduct-in-research case handling)**: **Not specified in official sources** (the procedure names the Registrar as the responsible officer, but does not state their formal reporting line). 

b. **Decision-making authority**

* **Registrar**:

  * Determines outcomes of the **preliminary review** (e.g., proceed to investigation / refer / dismiss) and issues a written determination. 
  * Decides whether to **set up an investigative Panel** and informs the Subject of that decision. 
  * After the Panel’s report, the Registrar **determines what further steps will be taken**, and **may dismiss the allegation or take further action** on receipt of the Panel’s recommendations. 
  * Takes **all decisions about disclosure** (what/when to disclose during the process). 

* **Panel (investigation stage)**:

  * Examines evidence and determines (on the balance of probabilities) whether misconduct occurred **in order to report to the Registrar**, and makes recommendations to the Registrar. 

* **Research and Innovation Committee**:

  * Has responsibility for policies promoting responsible conduct of research and **approves (on behalf of Council) the annual statement** of compliance with the Concordat to Support Research Integrity; it also **advises Council** on external influences. ([governance.admin.ox.ac.uk][1])

c. **Appeal or second-opinion mechanisms**

* **Students (as “Subject” in misconduct-in-research cases)**:

  * Right of appeal to a **Pro-Vice-Chancellor (PVC)** within **10 working days** of receiving the **Registrar’s decision** following a Panel investigation; appeals are **sent to the Registrar**, who identifies a PVC to consider the appeal. 
  * After completion of University procedures (Completion of Procedures letter), students may submit a complaint to the **Office of the Independent Adjudicator for Higher Education (OIA)** within **12 months**. 

* **Staff (as “Subject” in misconduct-in-research cases)**:

  * Right of appeal **under Statute XII** (for those subject to it) or **under the University formal disciplinary procedure for support staff** (if further action is taken under that procedure).

* **Annual research integrity reports** page (RIC approval of annual statement as delegated by Council). ([University of Oxford][2])
