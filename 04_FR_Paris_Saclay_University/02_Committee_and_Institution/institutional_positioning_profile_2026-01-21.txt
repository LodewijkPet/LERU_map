1. Official name of the unit
   Conseil pour l’éthique de la recherche et l’Intégrité scientifique de l’Université Paris-Saclay (POLÉTHIS). ([Université Paris-Saclay][1])

2. Institutional location
   Placed with the university Presidency (“placé auprès de la présidence”). ([Université Paris-Saclay][2])

3. Core responsibilities

* Handling academic misconduct cases
  Explicitly mentioned (for research/scientific integrity breaches):

  * The scientific integrity referents’ mission is to ensure (confidential) investigation/instruction of reported breaches. ([Université Paris-Saclay][3])
  * The network of “référents intégrité scientifique” within/around POLÉTHIS provides advice on dealing with cases of breaches, and the referent can be contacted confidentially for cases of scientific integrity breaches (e.g., plagiarism, falsification/fabrication, conflicts of interest). ([Université Paris-Saclay][4])

* Promoting academic integrity
  Explicitly mentioned:

  * POLÉTHIS is described as the university’s internal body coordinating efforts in scientific integrity and ethics and developing a shared culture of ethical engagement; it also provides training/support (e.g., ethics monitoring/consultation). ([Université Paris-Saclay][4])
  * The “réseau de référents intégrité scientifique” is described as aiming to inform/sensitise the community and to encourage good practices, including training-related contributions. ([Université Paris-Saclay][5])

* Overseeing integrity regulations / ordinance
  Not specified in official sources (no explicit statement found that POLÉTHIS “oversees” integrity regulations/ordinances as such). ([Université Paris-Saclay][1])

5. Reporting & authority structure

a. Reports to
POLÉTHIS is **placed under the authority of the President’s office / Presidency** (“Placed under the authority of the President’s office”; “Placé auprès de la Présidence”). 

b. Decision-making authority
**Advisory (explicitly stated).** Official university communications state that POLÉTHIS **advises the President’s office** and, upon request, **issues opinions/recommendations** (including for the Presidency, Governing Board and Academic Council), and **does not handle personal cases** (“sans toutefois traiter de cas personnels”). 
For the **scientific integrity referents network** (used for handling integrity breaches), official pages state that referents **confidentially “instruct”/investigate circumstances** of alleged breaches, but **final decision authority (e.g., sanctions) is not specified in official sources.** ([Université Paris-Saclay][1])

c. Appeal or second-opinion mechanisms
**Not specified in official sources.** (In the official pages consulted, no formal internal appeal route, second-opinion body, or escalation pathway is explicitly described for research integrity/misconduct decisions.)
