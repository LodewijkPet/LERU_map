I’ll look up Lund University’s official pages/regulations to identify the exact unit(s) handling research integrity/academic misconduct, confirm where they sit organizationally, and extract explicitly stated responsibilities with citations.


1. **Official name of the unit**
   **Deviations from Good Research Practice Review Board** (Swedish: **nämnden för utredning av avvikelse från god forskningssed**) 

2. **Institutional location**

* **Under the Vice-Chancellor (Rectorate / University management):** suspected deviations must be reported to the **Vice-Chancellor** or to the **Deviations from Good Research Practice Review Board**, and the Vice-Chancellor is the decision-maker in the process. ([staff.lu.se][1])
* **Administrative placement noted in governance document:** the university’s official guidelines list **“Organizational unit responsible: Office of the Vice-Chancellor”** (Swedish version: **“Ansvarig organisatorisk enhet: Universitetsledningens stab”**). 

3. **Core responsibilities**

* **Handling academic misconduct cases (explicitly mentioned):**

  * The Board **examines suspected deviations from good research practice** at Lund University and **initiates cases when a report is received**. 
  * The Board’s assignment is to **investigate and prepare the Vice-Chancellor’s decisions** regarding reported suspicions of *other* deviation from good research practice. 
  * If suspicions arise of **research misconduct (FFP)**, the Board must **transfer relevant documents to the National Board for Assessment of Research Misconduct (Npof)** (with authority delegated from the Vice-Chancellor). 

* **Promoting academic integrity (explicitly mentioned?):**
  **Not specified in official sources** as a responsibility of the *Deviations from Good Research Practice Review Board*. 

* **Overseeing integrity regulations / ordinance (explicitly mentioned):**
  The Board must examine deviations **in accordance with Chapter 1, Section 17 of the Higher Education Ordinance** and within the framework of the Swedish Act on Responsibility for Good Research Practice and the Examination of Research Misconduct (2019:504).

5. **Reporting & authority structure**

a. **Reports to**

* **Vice-Chancellor (Rector):** the Board’s assignment is to **“investigate and prepare Vice-Chancellor’s decisions”** in cases of suspected *other* deviation from good research practice, and **all members are appointed by the Vice-Chancellor**. 
* **External reporting obligation to the national body (Npof):** the Board is responsible for **annual reporting** of the university’s reviews of deviations from good research practice to the **National Board for Assessment of Research Misconduct (Npof)**. 

b. **Decision-making authority**

* **Other deviation from good research practice (local cases):** the Board investigates and proposes; **“The Vice-Chancellor makes the decision on the case.”** ([staff.lu.se][1])
* **Suspected research misconduct (FFP):** if suspicions of research misconduct arise, the Board must **decide to transfer the documents** to Npof (authority **delegated from the Vice-Chancellor**). 
* **Research misconduct decisions (national level):** under Swedish law, Npof’s examination is **reported in a decision** (“ska redovisas i ett beslut”). ([Riksdagen][2])
* **Conflict-of-interest safeguard (processing authority):** if a **member of University Management or the Board** is reported, the **University Board must decide how the case will be processed**. 

c. **Appeal or second-opinion mechanisms**

* **Second-opinion / external pathway (explicit):** suspected **research misconduct (FFP)** may be **reported directly to Npof**, and if misconduct suspicions arise during a Lund University investigation, the case materials are transferred to Npof. ([staff.lu.se][1])
* **Appeal of Npof decisions (explicit in law):** **decisions under Section 9** (and certain decisions under Section 12) **may be appealed to the general administrative courts** (“får överklagas till allmän förvaltningsdomstol”). ([Riksdagen][2])
* **Appeal of the Vice-Chancellor’s decision at Lund University:** **Not specified in official sources.**
